Prototype Summary

This prototype is to demonstrate what I have learned about color and type and how important they can help distinguish the "vibe" of a project at a glance. Richard Bright’s fictional campaign uses a measured, civic palette and editorial type to suggest open, dependable leadership. Secrets-o-pedia uses near-black surfaces, and file-like mono headings to make its "creepy-pasta" collection feel like you're entering a space you were never meant to find.

Richard Bright campaign

Palette and type choices

  • Campaign navy, #17324D: signals strength and civic trust and anchors the masthead.
  • Field paper, #F6F3EA: recalls printed town notices and gives policies a calm ground.
  • Slate ink, #243746: keeps everyday campaign text simple and distinct from the banner.
  • Sun gold, #F2C14E: gives the campaign call to action an optimistic, visible highlight that portrays importance.

Lora gives the candidate’s name and section headings an editorial, official feel; Source Sans 3 keeps service details easy to scan. Both use system font fallbacks.

Palette and type choices

  • Night index, #15171D: gives a dark secretive vibe, as well as making the page more condusive to nightime reading than out in the sunlight.
  • File bone, #EEE6D3: gives long entries a softer reading color than pure white and contrasting well with "Night Index".
  • Signal amber, #DBB45A: gives a sense of danger to links and headings.
  • Alert red, #CD5148: much like "Signal Amber" it gives a sense of danger, as well as a spooky vibe.

IBM Plex Mono makes titles and file labels feel machine-indexed, and gives the site a retro feel that suggests a long history on the web; system-ui keeps the text familiar and readable while also suggesting perhaps an amateurish nature to the site's coding. Both font families have generic fallbacks.
